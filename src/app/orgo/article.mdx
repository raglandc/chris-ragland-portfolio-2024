## My process

**1. Watch her videos and take notes ASAP.**

The sooner you get exposed to the material the better. I do not go crazy with notes, for me its making sure I understand what she is saying and can follow along. I create Anki cards as she goes (which I will share as I make them if you are interested, more about that later).

**2. Fail fast and often**

If you can, do practice problems after watching the video. The sooner you get something wrong and can figure out where your gaps are the better. **We learn much more from our mistakes than we do from our wins!** (This will always be true. Even when you future doctors start residency. You will fail but its better as a student than the attending. The important thing is that you learn from your mistakes.)

I generally try to do practice problems every week after I watch all the videos (see step 1).

**3. Review the vocab (the ideas)**

When I watch videos I feel that I say, "Oh well yeah that makes sense," and feel like I have the information on lock. I do in fact **not** have it on lock lol. This is why Anki is helpful. I use her slides and notes to make Anki cards and review them throughout the day. Generally while I am at the gym or sometimes before bed instead of doom scrolling.

Remember, _"Repetition is the mother of skill" - Someone_

I am still shocked by how fast I can forget something. Anki helps me keep seeing the stuff I saw in the videos. This helps move data from short term memory to long term memory. When exam time comes, it should be like a muscle reflex. You should vary rarely have to dig in your mind for the data.

I do these things each week. Falling behind is not an option. If you do not stay on top of the material it will stay on top of you until you are barried and cannot even stick your head up for air (too graphic?).

## Advice

**1. Her videos and notes should be your bible.**

She writes the tests and grades your work. She tells you exactly what she is looking for in the videos and on her notes. Be grateful, there are professors who are not so kind. Ask anyone who has taken courses at USF or some other major university. We are lucky, Dr. M is a real one. She wants us to succeed.

To be honest, I have not even been using the book for reading just practice problems. If there is something I am unsure on I _might_ look at the book but I probably ask an AI model to explain it to me like I am five.

**2. Treat the homework as a test**

I do not have much time to do many practice problems so I use the homework and really try to challenge my self and use it to check my understanding of the material. If I do not know something or recall how something is done I review it. This goes back to fail fast and often.

**3. Use her feedback to your advantage**

I cannot even believe she does this. Absolute legend. She is pretty much telling you where you are failing. Is that not so fire? I will say it again, absolute legend!

## Wtf is Anki?

Anki is a flash card application that uses the idea of spaced repetition. Instead of just showing you the same cards over and over it shows you the cards you actually need to review and less of the ones you do not need to review. This saves you so much time and brain power.

Also it is **FREE**

There is a phone app version that costs $25 for lifetime access but you do not have to buy it. You can do it all on your desktop or on their website. Just create an account to keep track of your notes across devices.

Link to check out Anki [here](https://apps.ankiweb.net/)

Once you have that installed, feel free to use my anki cards (download links at the bottom). I will update as I make the decks.
